# 👥 Cohort Analysis

🌐 [Русская версия](../topics/09-cohort-analysis.md)

**Cohort analysis** — a research method in which users are grouped into **cohorts** by a common characteristic (usually the date of first activity) and tracked over time.

**Example of cohorts:**
- Users who signed up in January — cohort 1.
- Users who signed up in February — cohort 2.
- and so on.

**What is typically measured across cohorts:**
- **Retention** — the share of the cohort that remains active in subsequent months (see [Retention](08-retention.md)).
- **Revenue per cohort** — how the cohort "brings in" money over time.
- **Conversion** to paying users.

**The main question cohort analysis answers:**
"Is the behavior of new users getting better (or worse) compared to previous cohorts?"

**Example:**
The January cohort retains 40% of users into month 2, February — 45%, March — 48%. Conclusion: the product is improving, users value it more.

If retention is falling instead — the product (or marketing) is degrading, and this is visible before the problems show up in financial results.

**Difference from "time slices":**
- A typical report "average retention across all users" mixes newcomers and long-time users — misleading.
- Cohort analysis isolates the behavior of specific groups and correctly tracks the dynamics.

**Practical applications:**
- Assessing the impact of releases (comparing cohorts before and after a feature).
- LTV planning (forecasting based on the behavior of early cohorts).
- Diagnosing the causes of growth/decline in key metrics.

**Insight:** cohort analysis is the key tool for assessing retention and the effectiveness of product changes. Without it, any claim "retention has grown" has no evidence base.
